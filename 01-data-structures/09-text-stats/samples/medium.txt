Trading in financial markets is fundamentally an exercise in probability. Every position a trader takes carries an expected value, a distribution of possible outcomes, and a measure of risk. The best traders are not the ones who are right most often; they are the ones who size their bets correctly and manage their downside. A strategy with a positive edge can still lose money if the variance is poorly understood. This is why mental arithmetic, quick estimation, and a firm grasp of statistics matter so much. Speed without accuracy is worthless, and accuracy without speed is often too slow to be useful in a live market.
